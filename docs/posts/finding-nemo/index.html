Finding Nemo

Trees
September 15, 2026 · 3 min · 630 words · Minhaj U. Khan

Running. It has become something that I just do, now.

To challenge this idea that my brain keeps convincing me of, I registered to run a half marathon in October, and put myself to the test.

I read an article last Thursday about writing better. A guiding principle to write something that captivates readers is as follows: before you start writing a word, you must be sure of one thing: the trunk of the tree. The trunk of the tree is the intent of the text. The author must be well aware of the central idea intended to be conveyed to the reader. The article also continued to draw parallels about branches, leaves and fruits of the trees with something important, but how unfortunate, for you reading this, I have forgotten about that part.

It has been four years that I’ve moved to Berlin for a new job. I still work at the same company, though throughout the years, a lot has changed. The company went through phases of growth, hiring, acquisitions, layoffs, cringefests, more layoffs, some hirings again, AI. The usuals in tech.

Training for the half has been mostly consistent, in comparison to the events at my day job. Even though some of the workouts fell through the cracks, due to illness, I still try to keep the required mileage numbers in order. I have been entertaining this thought lately, that Running consistently has been filling in a void – something that I have missed in these four years in a foreign land: a sense of belonging, real relationships, fun. Emotions that I am unable to put into words, hence the selection of word: fun.

The branches emerging from the trunk of the trees are supposed to be arguments, facts, statistics – I think. I could be wrong.

Instead of all that, let’s start with an opinion: Working as a Software Engineer was more fun before AI took over. Writing the code by hand felt like craft. Typing each character of syntax left marks in the ever-shrinking memories with short attention spans. The bigger problem, however, is how this technology is devouring natural resources, just so we could find less joy in our jobs as engineers and be more what we despise: a product manager.

The harsh reality is that we can’t go back. Or can we?

In the pursuit of a sense of belonging, real relationships, and most importantly, the fun - I travelled back to Karachi in November last year. What I was searching for was present, but only partly. Same roads, same restaurants, same potholes from twenty-three years ago. Not much had changed, except the people. They had moved to different countries, became busy parents, or were just struggling in the deadly 9 - 5 in this tough economy.

Well, It’s not as sadistic as it sounds. Some of the old folk did take the time out, and when they did, we would succeed in Finding Nemo.

I spent about 4 - 5 months in Karachi, having fun and not at the same time. At the times of “not”, supplemented by a lot of free hours at hand, I tried to fill it with running again. There was no consistency, of course. Additionally, the numbers would be super disapointing. I couldn’t run a 5k with easy pace without my heart rate shooting high. Today, after almost 6 months of being back at Running, I ran fifteen kilometers, and to my surprise, 75% of the distance I ran was at the pace I want to run the half marathon at – and it didn’t feel too hard!

To end with the fruit from the tree:

“Pain is inevitable. Suffering is optional" - Haruki Murakami

I leave it to you to carve its name on the trunk.
