SportScheck Half Marathon 2026

Getting back to running, training for four months, and crossing the finish line of my first half marathon.
October 19, 2026 · 4 min · 778 words · Minhaj U. Khan

Today, I registered myself for the Berlin Half Marathon in 2026 lottery. I hoped I would get in, finish it, and come back to this blog to appreciate and be grateful for how far I had come. A man can dream!

This is from a piece I wrote on 5 April 2025 when I ran my first 10K.

I didn’t get into the Berlin Half Marathon for April 2026. Worse, I stopped running entirely after September 2025.

After coming back from a long vacation from November 2025 to March 2026, I found myself having gained a lot of weight. Smoking without restraint during that time, my resting heart rate had also become higher than usual. I had moments of realisation about my worsening fitness when I was still back in Karachi, but did not manage to find the time or motivation to get back to running. Getting out to run after work late at night and then driving to the track was just too much to do (it wasn’t too much — I was just lazy).

I had decided that I was going to get back to running as soon as I landed back in Berlin. I was out for a run the same week.

Even though I had run 10k before, running only 2–3 kilometres, even at a slower pace, was challenging. I went on with a walk/run program with Runna, and in six weeks I was back to running 5k.

On 30 July, one ambitious day, I registered for a half marathon in November. I thought to myself that it would be a nice challenge to take on, and I had around four months to train for it.

During the training block, some days felt amazing; on other days, it felt like I had made no progress at all, even though I had been running for weeks. Nonetheless, the block was no short of happy moments: I ran a sub-30-minute 5k twice, ran with friends for Wings for Life, took my little one (Umar) for a Parkrun (we cut it short to 3k), and ran all the way from Spandau to Berlin Central Station.

The training block was certainly FULL OF MEMORIES.

Race day#

The weather was not friendly, at least initially. It was cold (10 degrees) and raining.

Being out in the cold and wet 30 minutes before the race had me needing a visit to the bathroom. I was standing at the start line already wanting to pee. NGL, it crossed my mind that maybe I should go to the bathroom mid-race.

My race pace was supposed to be somewhere around 6:40 minutes/km, and for the first three kilometres I kept the same pace. I was feeling pretty good, and then decided to push myself, maintaining a pace between 6:10–6:25 for the entire race.

Within 10 minutes of the race, I was completely soaked. I could feel my socks entirely wet and every step of the run felt like I was stepping in a puddle. The good part: free showers kept my body temperature cooler. I did not feel any exhaustion for, I think I can say, 70% of the entire race.

My friend Mr Saraogi, who agreed to help me on this endeavour, played his part to perfection in pacing me. It wouldn’t have been possible without his support throughout the run: keeping it chill, not letting me lose focus, avoiding negative talk, and checking on me to see if I was doing okay and could push through.

Looking back at it, the most fun part of the race was the last six kilometres. Till the fourth kilometre out of six, I pushed hard and tried to maintain a pace between 6:05–6:15. The goal was to hold the pace till the end of the race, but it kept getting harder and harder. The last two kilometres felt like they would never end.

At one point I was almost going to give up. However, the silent, positive self-talk at that moment was what all the money and training were for. My legs had given up, my calves were cramping, and I had a swollen toe — but I forced myself to push through and reminded myself that if I didn’t do it now, I would regret not giving it 100% after all this time put to work.

The emotions after crossing that finish line were two: relief that it was over, and pride. Pride that I had done a half marathon — and at a pace I didn’t expect myself to run at.

After receiving the medal, refuelling with a banana and bread, and taking pictures, I finally peed in the bathroom — peacefully.
